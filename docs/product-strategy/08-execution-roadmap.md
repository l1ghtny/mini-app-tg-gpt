# Execution roadmap

The sequence protects the launch from a common failure mode: buying traffic before the product can explain its value, establish trust, measure activation, and convert a browser user reliably.

## Phase 0: decisions and baselines — days 1–3

- Accept, revise, or reject the focused research/document positioning.
- Confirm the master brand and control of the canonical domain.
- Snapshot current traffic, activation, retention, and payment metrics.
- Assign owners for product, SEO/editorial, design, analytics, trust/legal, and support.
- Create a public-claim register and verify all current landing claims.
- Define “activated user” and the first successful task event.

Exit condition: the team has one position, one canonical domain plan, named owners, and baseline metrics.

## Phase 1: browser launch foundation — days 1–14

### Product and platform

- Deploy and E2E-test browser auth, callback/CORS/SMTP configuration, logout, and recovery.
- Harden browser session storage and account-linking flows.
- Expose current pricing and model catalogue publicly.
- Fix corrupted Sonar fallback text and audit user-visible encoding.
- Verify browser send, stream, reconnect, resume, document, image, entitlement, and payment flows.

### Website and trust

- Move canonical, sitemap, robots, and metadata to the chosen domain.
- Publish privacy, terms, offer, refunds, file processing, contacts, and account deletion/linking information after review.
- Add real product screenshots or a short recording.
- Put exact price/free allowance/payment information near the first CTA.
- Install full-funnel analytics, Search Console, Yandex Webmaster, and Yandex Metrica.

Exit condition: a new visitor can understand the offer, verify the operator and price, authenticate, complete a task, and pay in a measured production flow.

## Phase 2: activation and proof — weeks 3–6

- Add a safe guest demo or free first-value path.
- Transfer prompts/templates from SEO pages into the application.
- Publish `/pricing`, `/models`, core feature pages, help, status, and changelog.
- Add role-based onboarding and three real end-to-end workflow demos.
- Collect genuine reviews and two permissioned customer case studies.
- Publish the first 8–10 original-evidence articles from two priority clusters.
- Add model availability, task guidance, and representative cost information.
- Test hero message, proof placement, price placement, and authentication friction.

Exit condition: the team can identify which landing intent produces activated and retained users, not only clicks.

## Phase 3: focused differentiation — weeks 6–12

- Release deterministic automatic model recommendations with explanations and cost estimates.
- Add two- or three-model side-by-side comparison.
- Integrate Claude after commercial, policy, billing, and reliability review.
- Improve document citations, OCR/table status, multi-document projects, and exports.
- Make the subscription-plus-top-up model transparent.
- Launch referral and lifecycle programs with fraud and consent controls.
- Publish repeatable model, citation, and cost benchmarks.

Exit condition: the product has a demonstrable research/document advantage and improving week-four retention among the target cohort.

## Phase 4: selective expansion — months 4–6

- Add DeepSeek or Qwen if demand and unit economics support it.
- Build reusable personal workflows and project memory.
- Validate team/business demand through interviews and paid pilots.
- Consider API access only after metering, quotas, documentation, and support are ready.
- Expand image editing or other media based on observed paid usage.

## Work deliberately deferred

- Large-scale video/music provider integrations.
- An enterprise suite before individual retention and trust foundations are proven.
- Dozens of generic SEO pages without original evidence.
- A model-count race.
- Advanced autonomous agents without validated workflows.

## Operating cadence

Weekly:

- review funnel and provider reliability;
- inspect top search queries and landing-to-activation performance;
- verify public pricing/catalogue accuracy;
- choose one conversion or retention experiment;
- review user-support evidence and failed tasks.

Monthly:

- update competitor and model catalogue snapshots;
- publish one original benchmark or reliability report;
- review gross margin by model and workflow;
- revisit roadmap priorities using activation and retention evidence;
- audit public claims and policy accuracy.

## Immediate backlog

1. Decide Lightny AI and canonical domain architecture.
2. Finish production browser-auth and account-linking verification.
3. Make plans and model access public.
4. Complete the legal/trust page set.
5. Add product proof and full-funnel measurement.
6. Build the guest/demo first-task path.
7. Begin model routing and document citation improvements.
8. Add Claude, then evaluate DeepSeek or Qwen.
