# Concluding promise, viability, and customer value

Last updated: 2026-07-27

## Executive conclusion

Lightny AI should not try to win by having the largest model catalogue or the lowest theoretical price per token. It should win by removing tariff mathematics from serious AI work.

The defensible promise is:

> **AI without token math. For text, one completed answer uses one clear request. Choose the level of intelligence, see exactly what remains, and continue the same work in the browser or Telegram.**

That promise becomes meaningfully different when it is combined with sourced research, document work, automatic model guidance, reliable completion, and continuity between a full web workspace and Telegram.

The available billing evidence indicates that the business is more financially viable than the first token reconstruction suggested. The complete OpenAI project spent $11.84 over the supplied 30-day dashboard window; the earlier claim that one user cost more than $20 was invalid because it ignored cached-input pricing.

The conclusion is therefore:

- keep request-based subscriptions;
- do not expose tokens or provider cost to customers;
- repair internal cost telemetry and add invisible per-response safeguards;
- keep current prices and request allowances while exact cached-token and provider-cost telemetry is added;
- add simple request packs so occasional and burst users are not forced into a universal credit wallet;
- compete on completed work and predictability, not raw compute resale.

## What is genuinely differentiated

### The category promise

Most Russian aggregators make the customer understand an internal currency or provider economics:

- Chad uses sparks whose consumption depends on request words, answer words, conversation context, files, and model multipliers;
- BotHub uses Caps and publishes token-level prices plus per-request and tool charges;
- GPTunnel charges a ruble balance by input and output tokens;
- Neuromia uses non-expiring credits and shows a generation price before launch.

Lightny can instead make the commercial unit an outcome the customer understands: one completed text answer. Model groups—Fast, Smart, Balanced, and Flagship—communicate quality and scarcity without asking the customer to calculate tokens, context length, or exchange rates.

This is not merely simpler pricing copy. It should influence the whole product:

- the task starts with the desired result, not a catalogue of model IDs;
- the app recommends the appropriate intelligence level;
- the user sees the request pool and remaining answers before sending;
- failed generations are refunded;
- expensive technical complexity remains an internal routing and cost-control concern;
- the answer can be researched, continued, organized, and reused across browser and Telegram.

### What is not differentiated yet

Russian payment support, access without a foreign card, a Russian interface, GPT and Gemini access, image generation, files, and a Telegram bot are market expectations. Claude, model comparison, polished proof, legal information, and a guest experience are important parity gaps, not unique positioning.

The product only earns its promise when the web workspace, account continuity, research sources, document citations, exports, request accounting, and failure recovery work reliably in production.

## Financial viability

### Where the economics work

Observed production personas and the provider dashboard show a favorable starting position:

- the entire OpenAI project cost $11.84 in the supplied 30-day window;
- low-frequency and Fast-first users are structurally cheaper than Flagship-heavy users;
- context squashing reduces repeated-input cost and makes longer conversations more affordable;
- subscriptions create predictable revenue and unused allowances provide natural breakage.

If the majority of paying users resemble the steady utility persona, Basic and Advanced can produce healthy blended margin.

### Where uncertainty remains

Provider cost still varies, and the current request cap is not a complete internal financial control:

- one request can produce a short answer or a long reasoning response;
- one answer can invoke web search repeatedly;
- context can still reach 60-80k tokens at p90 despite squashing;
- high-quality images are separately expensive;
- the database currently loses the cached-input and cache-write split needed to price those requests accurately.

The product appears viable at current scale and usage. Exact tier margin remains unknown until OpenAI, Google, Perplexity, image, infrastructure, and payment costs are reconciled per billing cycle.

### Commercial launch gates

Before final public pricing:

1. Correct exact provider-cost telemetry for tokens, cached tokens, searches, files, images, retries, and every active model.
2. Reconcile internal totals against provider billing exports.
3. Add output, reasoning, context, search-call, image, and automation safeguards.
4. Measure at least 30 genuinely paying users for a complete billing cycle.
5. Require positive contribution from every tier at p90 behavior and target at least 50-60% blended contribution after provider-variable cost and payment fees.

The exact margin target is a commercial decision, but a tier that is predictably negative for an engaged user is not an acquisition strategy; it is a pricing defect.

## Recommended pricing decision

### Keep

- Basic at 490 RUB as the acquisition and everyday-use tier while the paid cohort is measured.
- Advanced at 1,490 RUB as the mainstream research and professional tier.
- Request pools instead of tokens or universal credits.
- Generous Fast and Smart access, protected by human-use rate and automation controls.
- Existing friend subscriptions as research cohorts; do not silently reduce their access.

### Correct before unrestricted launch

- Keep Premium at 2,490 RUB with its current 100 Flagship allowance during measurement; there is no verified cost basis for cutting it.
- Store cached-input and cache-write tokens and reconcile against the OpenAI Costs endpoint or dashboard.
- Treat a future Power tier as a demand and packaging opportunity, not an emergency margin fix.
- Limit ordinary web-enabled answers to a reasonable number of searches. Give genuinely research-intensive work its own understandable Deep Research answer allowance.
- Replace image-energy jargon with estimated numbers of low, medium, and high-quality generations while retaining energy internally.

Basic and Advanced Balanced allowances may also need adjustment, but there is not enough paid-cohort evidence to choose defensible final numbers today. Per-response safeguards and accurate measurement should come before arbitrary reductions.

### Expand the value ladder

- A useful free task or guest preview for acquisition.
- Non-token request packs for occasional users and temporary bursts.
- Premium and Power differentiation based on intelligence level, research depth, documents, and workflow—not merely more messages.
- Annual plans only after retention is measured.
- Optional capped rollover can be tested later if monthly expiry becomes a conversion objection; do not add it before modeling the concentrated cost risk.

## Do users actually get better value?

### Yes, for the intended customer

A recurring knowledge worker gets better value when they care about:

- knowing how many useful answers remain without calculating tokens or credits;
- choosing task quality rather than learning model pricing;
- researched answers with sources;
- working repeatedly with documents and organized conversations;
- moving between a browser workspace and Telegram;
- reliable completion and refunds for failed generations.

For this customer, value is reduced decision effort and completed work—not the lowest ruble price per million tokens.

### Not universally

- An occasional user making a handful of cheap requests may spend less with GPTunnel, BotHub, or Neuromia pay-as-you-go.
- A user who wants dozens of image, video, music, and voice models gets more breadth from Chad or Neuromia.
- A team requiring API access, SSO, invoicing, and administrative controls gets more immediate value from established business offerings.
- Until browser/Telegram identity, citations, exports, legal pages, proof, and onboarding are finished, Lightny's workflow advantage is still partly a roadmap claim.

Therefore, do not advertise “the cheapest AI” or “the most models.” Advertise less tariff anxiety and a clearer route from question to reusable result.

## What to expand next

### Differentiation first

1. Automatic task-to-model guidance with a clear request-pool impact.
2. Research mode with source-quality controls, navigable citations, and reusable research results.
3. Document collections, page-level citations, comparison, and export to Markdown, DOCX, and PDF.
4. Verified browser-Telegram linking, shared history, prompt handoff, and continuation.
5. Request packs and a clear Premium/Power value ladder.

### Parity second

1. Claude integration.
2. Side-by-side model comparison.
3. Guest/free first-value experience.
4. Trust, legal, deletion, retention, status, and public catalogue surfaces.
5. Real screenshots, benchmark workflows, user stories, and verified reviews.

### Later

- broad video and music generation;
- a model-count race;
- team/enterprise commitments before individual retention is proven;
- autonomous-agent complexity before the core research workflow converts.

## Final strategic statement

Lightny AI can offer better value than Russian AI aggregators, but only to a deliberately chosen customer: someone who uses AI repeatedly for research, documents, writing, and technical work and values predictable answers over token arithmetic.

The request-based subscription is the differentiator and should remain. Its simplicity must be protected by sophisticated internal cost accounting, routing, and safeguards. The product should be more opinionated than competitors—fewer model decisions, clearer research results, stronger continuity—and more honest about where it is not yet superior.

The winning position is not “more neural networks for fewer rubles.” It is:

> **Less tariff math. Better completed work. One clear workspace in the browser and Telegram.**
