# Competitive landscape

Snapshot date: 2026-07-26. Competitor features and prices are volatile; re-check primary websites before using these facts in public comparisons.

## Competitive baseline

Today’s credible Russian aggregator usually offers:

- access without foreign payment cards and often without a VPN;
- public prices and a free starting allowance;
- several text models plus image generation;
- browser-first onboarding;
- examples, screenshots, testimonials, and visible trust signals;
- legal documents and a recognizable payment flow;
- enough model breadth to cover GPT, Claude, Gemini, and at least one lower-cost alternative.

These capabilities are not sufficient differentiation, but missing them creates conversion friction.

## Where AI with UI is already credible

- Strong backend reliability patterns: idempotent billable operations, entitlement checks, resumable SSE streams, and explicit chain-state handling.
- A focused but useful catalogue covering GPT, Gemini, Perplexity search, and multiple image models.
- Search depth choices, URL retrieval, finance-related search, documents, images, folders, sharing, and personalization.
- A Telegram user base and delivery surface that can become a companion to the browser product.
- Subscription, usage-pack, discount, and Russian payment foundations.
- Canary deployment and Sentry observability that support safer iteration.

These are product strengths, but most are invisible to a buyer. Reliability must be translated into customer benefits such as “the answer continues after reconnecting” or “you will not be charged twice,” only where those claims can be supported.

## Competitive gaps

### High impact

- Claude is absent from the public model story.
- No obvious automatic model recommendation or task router.
- No side-by-side answer comparison.
- Cost is not estimated clearly before a request.
- Public pricing and value are not visible enough before authentication.
- Trust, legal, file-processing, and data-deletion information is incomplete.
- The website lacks strong product proof: screenshots, real outputs, user stories, and verified reviews.
- Browser onboarding still has deployment and end-to-end verification work.

### Medium impact

- DeepSeek or Qwen could provide a recognized economical alternative after Claude.
- Document workflows need page-level citations, OCR/table confidence, multi-document workspaces, and exports.
- Reusable prompt templates and workflows are underdeveloped.
- Business/team controls, API access, and a public status page are future credibility layers.

## Competitive stance

Use competitors as category educators, not as the main identity. Comparison pages should be factual, dated, respectful, and based on repeatable tests. The best contrast is not “we have more models”; it is:

- fewer decisions before useful work;
- stronger sources and document handling;
- clearer cost;
- continuity between the full browser workspace and Telegram;
- evidence from original benchmarks and real workflows.
