# Landing and conversion

## The landing page’s job

The landing page should move a qualified visitor from recognition to a first useful result. It is not a complete feature inventory. The first screen must explain the category, outcome, differentiator, commercial starting point, and next action.

## Recommended page sequence

### 1. Hero

Include:

- one outcome-led headline;
- one clarifying sentence naming GPT, Gemini, AI search, files, browser, and Telegram where natural;
- a primary browser CTA such as “Try in the browser”;
- a secondary Telegram CTA;
- a visible free starting allowance or honest price anchor;
- a real interface image or short product recording.

Do not show two equally weighted CTAs. Browser activation is primary; Telegram is the companion choice.

### 2. Immediate proof

Show three short, real workflows rather than abstract feature cards:

1. Research a question and inspect sources.
2. Upload a PDF and navigate page citations.
3. Compare or route a task to the appropriate model.

Use real inputs and outputs, note the model/date, and avoid cherry-picked claims that cannot be repeated.

### 3. Why this product

Explain the four differentiators: reliable sources, document work, model guidance with cost, and web–Telegram continuity. Each block should link to a deeper feature page.

### 4. Model and capability proof

Show a curated catalogue grouped by job: fast everyday work, deep reasoning, research/search, images. Link to a live model page containing availability, limits, and update dates.

### 5. Role-based use cases

Use outcome pages for analysts, developers, marketers, managers, and students. Each page should contain a workflow, a realistic example, limits, and a direct handoff into the app.

### 6. Pricing

Display exact current prices, free allowance, renewal, payment methods, and what each plan enables. Compare plans by outcomes and likely usage rather than only internal credits.

### 7. Trust

Show the legal operator, support response expectations, payment processor, data/file handling summary, deletion controls, genuine reviews, and service status. Only state “without VPN,” storage location, or encryption properties after verification.

### 8. FAQ and final CTA

Answer purchasing objections: models, payment, cancellation, files, data, Telegram linking, free use, and support. FAQ remains useful for people even where search engines do not grant a special rich result.

## Missing conversion mechanisms

- Guest or no-risk first task.
- Yandex ID and/or simple email authentication in addition to Telegram, where technically and commercially appropriate.
- Application-side consumption of the `prompt` handoff already emitted by SEO pages.
- Correct return to the originating page or task after login.
- A pricing page that works without authentication.
- An onboarding choice based on the user’s job, followed by a relevant sample workflow.
- Honest usage and cost preview before a high-cost action.
- Captured reason when a user abandons purchase or cancels.

## Copy rules

- Lead with what the user can complete, not implementation details.
- Use specific proof and dates instead of “best,” “fastest,” or “most powerful.”
- Separate current capability from “coming soon.”
- Avoid claiming every listed model is always available; expose live status where possible.
- Explain credits or energy in rubles and representative tasks.
- Use native Russian editing for all published Russian prose.

## Landing acceptance criteria

- A new visitor understands the product in five seconds.
- Pricing and the free starting point are reachable without login.
- The primary CTA opens a working browser flow and preserves campaign attribution.
- Every screenshot reflects the current product.
- All major claims have an owner and verification source.
- Landing-to-app, authentication, first task, and payment events are measured.
